## EP004: The Black Death Ended. Suddenly You Could Name Your Wage (topic 7, money pillar, score 84)
Why this one:
- **Rule 10.** England, 1349 to 1381. EP001 was Egypt 2560 BC, EP002 New York 1929, EP003 Roman Italy AD 79: a different era and country from all three. Money pillar; EP003 was point of view.
- **Score.** Tied top of `topic-next` at 84 with "Your Life Savings in 1923 Germany". 1923 is six years from EP002's 1929 and the same kind of story (savings destroyed), so it fails the different-era test in spirit. "A Roman's day" (83) is Rome again. "A house in 2006" (83) and the Gold Rush (82) are the United States again.
- **Evidence.** Medieval England is on both outlier lists; money stories are the biggest videos on the nearest competitor (last run's pull).
- **Our own Shorts.** The three that were shown to anyone each stated one concrete fact. This story has at least five: the reaper who wanted eightpence against a law that said two; fines that paid the village's tax; a wage crossed out in an account roll; a sick-leave list from 1349; the day labourer who doubled his pay while the ploughman waited thirty years.
- **It can be checked.** The statute text, a chronicle, and three recent papers were all open to read.
Passed over: the Vikings-in-winter idea (81) fits the season but demand for it was not measured; kept for December.

Working title: **You Survived the Black Death. Now Ask for a Raise.** (final title and thumbnail are run B's job.)

## What the research changed
The plan's premise ("suddenly you could name your wage") is half true, and the episode is now built on the other half. Day labourers nearly doubled their rate in a year. Ploughmen on yearly contracts, the lead character's job, saw their pay stay flat on average until about 1380 (Claridge, Delabastita and Gibbs, 2023), and prices stayed high until the mid-1370s (Routt). That is the hidden fourth quest.
