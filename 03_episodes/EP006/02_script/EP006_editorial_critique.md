## Pass 1: v1 against the sources

### The premise was wrong
1. **v1's ending says winter is when the sail gets made ("now you know what winter was for").** No source opened says spinning or weaving was winter work. One says the opposite: "A task women performed year round was the making of cloth." The hidden quest is rebuilt on what the sources do say: cloth-making never stops, the sail is the largest single piece of it, and the people who made it are not the ones the age is named after.
2. **Line 81, "the wool of two hundred sheep", and line 82, "four years of one woman's full-time work".** Five sources give five answers. Wool: 150 to 200 sheep (University of Bergen), "as many as 200" (Viking Ship Museum, read as a summary only), about 700 (the weaver Amy Lightfoot, for a sail she made). Labour: about four years of full-time work (Bergen); four women for a year at ten hours a day (Eva Andersson Strand, from experiments); two skilled women for a year (Lightfoot); 7,850 hours of weaving alone (the museum). The script now gives the range for the wool and one named kind of estimate for the work, and says "by that count" when it uses it again. Andersson Strand also says one woman alone for four years would not be practical, so "one woman" is cut.
3. **Line 87, "By her sums, every man on a rowing bench needs a woman at home".** The sentence is on a news site reporting Lise Bender Jørgensen's figures and may be the site's own gloss. Her abstract, quoted on the same site, gives 50 to 60 years for a warship of 65 to 70 men; the 84 years is for a larger ship and 100 men. Kept as "one calculation" and "by that sum", with nobody named.

### Unsupported or overstated
4. Line 1, "midwinter ... four and a half hours": the 4 h 30 min figure is the solstice at Trondheim. Snorri's "midwinter night" may be in January, when the day is longer. The opening now says late December; midwinter is kept for the calendar's halfway point.
5. Line 2, "Winter here lasts a hundred and eighty days": that is the Icelandic calendar, not attested before the 12th century. Say whose count it is.
6. Line 2, "Everything you'll eat until spring is already inside this house": nothing supports "everything". York's winter foods include shellfish. Now: what the farm killed and stored has to last.
7. Line 4, "beside the Trondheim fjord": Trondheim did not exist as a town in 950 and the fjord is not known to me to freeze, which the skating scene needs. "Central Norway."
8. Line 9, "the reason there is a Viking Age at all": a claim nobody can check. Replaced with a tease.
9. Line 11, "The Viking year has two seasons": Icelandic calendar. Attribute.
10. Line 13, "You have survived fifteen": invented and pointless. Cut.
11. Line 15, "Hay is the only thing your animals will eat until April": the Hrísbrú evidence is that cattle were kept in and sheep could graze. Say that, and say it is Iceland.
12. Line 18, "the cows starve in March": no source. Cut.
13. Line 19, "small farms kill most of the herd": Byock has "severely cull". "Most" is mine.
14. Line 20, "The hungry year is the one after": Byock, as read, has farmers after a second bad summer trying to keep what is left. Reworded to that.
15. Line 21, "if you can afford salt": no source on the price of salt. "Where there's salt."
16. Line 23, "sink the meat in vats of sour milk": the source says curdled milk was used as a preservative; it does not say meat.
17. Line 25, "It keeps for ten years": the source says some medieval accounts suggest as much as ten years.
18. Line 26, "The DNA said Lofoten": close, but the page says the bones matched the Arctic stock that swims off Lofoten, and that it cannot tell whether the fish was traded. Reworded.
19. Line 30, "five metres wide and twenty long", "fire trench": the museum page gives no dimensions. Two secondary pages give 5 to 7 by 15 to 75 metres. Dimensions cut; "long fire" is the museum's "long fireplace".
20. Line 32, "sitting up, with your back to the wall": one secondary page says "may have". Cut.
21. Line 33, "a box bed with a door": one secondary page. Cut.
22. Line 34, "They're radiators": the only source for this is a reporter's guess. The museum says animals were stalled at one end if there were no stables. Use that.
23. Line 36, "soot ... far above": the measurements are carbon monoxide and fine particles; the report says "significantly higher" than WHO standards.
24. Line 37, "They're the ones who never leave the house": my reason, not the study's. The study says women and small children probably had the highest exposure. Keep "probably", drop the reason.
25. Line 39, "a stone dish of fish oil": secondary pages only, and they disagree about the wick. The York page has the fire as the main light, with rush lamps and wax candles.
26. Line 41, "made with a single needle, in a stitch older than knitting": not in the source. The source has the technique's name and that it is the only one found in England.
27. Line 43, "A towel": that is the 1923 Bellows translation as reprinted in 1936. The 1908 Bray translation, which is the one read, has "drying". Paraphrased from Bray.
28. Line 46, "The Vikings called this month fat-sucker": an Icelandic month name, glossed that way by a newspaper. Attribute and soften.
29. Line 50, "across the fjord with a spiked pole": the museums say a pole; the spike is from a secondary page. The fjord is invented.
30. Line 52, "on one street": the source says at Coppergate, an excavation. "At one dig."
31. Line 54, "A pair of Viking skis ... wide as your hand is long": they are from about AD 700 or the later 700s, before the Viking Age. Say so. Drop the hand.
32. Line 56, "half past two, and you have nineteen hours to fill": the sunset time is a modern clock time on the shortest day; the nineteen hours is my arithmetic and ignores twilight. "In the middle of the afternoon."
33. Line 57, the rules of hnefatafl: not in the source. The museum says only that it was played.
34. Line 58, "So people gambled": an inference. First softened to a joke, then cut in pass 2.
35. Line 59, "in graves": the museum page does not say graves.
36. Line 60, "Every night somebody tells a story": the museum places storytelling at festive occasions.
37. Line 62, "a shepherd called Glam": the two pages opened do not say shepherd. "A man."
38. Line 65, "Yule begins on the longest night and lasts three days": the source is Snorri, about 1230; it is midwinter night and three nights; and the date is disputed. All three said.
39. Line 66, "Your king ... has just passed a law": Snorri's account, not a record. "He also says."
40. Line 67, "every man must brew sixteen litres of ale": the 16.2 litres is one estimate of the measure. Separated and attributed. (Pass 2 found a further problem here.)
41. Line 68, "the only law in history that people looked forward to obeying": a claim about all of history. Replaced with a joke that claims nothing.
42. Line 69, "in your district": the page says farmers from Þrándheimr. That this is the Trondheim region is my knowledge, not the page's. District left out.
43. Line 71, "Every Viking is a farmer first": no source opened says it. Cut.
44. Line 74, "the monks noticed it too": who kept the Chronicle is not in the source. Name the Chronicle.
45. Line 78, "By firelight": mine. Cut.
46. Line 86, "three percent": 3.4%. "About three percent."
47. Line 89, "Oak, iron, a dragon on the prow": no source opened. Cut.
