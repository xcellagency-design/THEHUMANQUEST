# EP004 editorial pass on v1 (hostile-editor read), 18 Nov 2026

Line numbers are 0-based rows of `EP004_script_v1.txt`. Every point was checked against a source opened in this run (see `01_facts/facts.json`).

## Unsupported or overstated

1. Line 0: "the most valuable thing in England" is a boast, not a fact. And the opening takes two lines to reach the stakes. Open on the year, the dead and the raise.
2. Line 1: "something like half the country" hides a real argument. Estimates opened this run run from about 20% (Russell) to 62.5% (Benedictow); 40 to 60% is the range most often quoted. Say that it is disputed.
3. Line 13: "crowded" is one historian's judgement (Ibeji: "horrendously overpopulated"). Attribute it.
4. Line 16: the penny-and-a-half day rate is the 1349 average in one data set, not a pre-plague rate. Move it to where the rise is described and say what it is.
5. Line 19: "for nothing" is wrong. Labour service was owed for the holding. The source says two or three days a week on the lord's land; say only that.
6. Line 20: the source opened says the heir paid an inheritance payment and that fines were often paid in kind, for example the best animal. It does not say the lord took the best animal at death. Also "usually" could not leave.
7. Line 21: "for centuries" was not checked. Cut.
8. Line 22: "and he's sick" is one chronicle's account (Grey Friars). Other chronicles name Bristol or Southampton. Attribute.
9. Line 23: the source says London certainly by November, possibly late September. Say November.
10. Line 30: the Warboys list is a list of survivors. Say so, or it reads as if most people recovered.
11. Line 34: "Nobody's left to buy it" is my explanation. Knighton's own is the general fear of death. Cut the gloss.
12. Line 39: Knighton does not say "a day". Two translations agree on 8d with food for a reaper and disagree on the mower (12d in Cheyney, 10d in Hughes), so the mower stays out.
13. Line 40: "Across England" claims too much. It is the average in one sample of manor accounts (Claridge, Delabastita and Gibbs): 0.13s in 1349, 0.25s in 1350.
14. Line 41: at Fornham more than half the reaping was still done at 3d an acre; only the rest cost 5d.
15. Line 43: "he won't ask where you came from" is invented colour. Cut.
16. Line 45: the roll explains the rise in stipends by the heavy mortality; the 6s is a separate extra payment and the source does not say who received it. Use the stipend rise (about 10%, most for ploughmen and the carter) and the food allowance (about 30%).
17. Line 54: keep, but it must rest on the text: the Ordinance punishes taking more than the customary wage. "Asking" comes from one secondary summary. Fold it into the cold open as the king "making that a crime" and drop the separate line.
18. Line 67: the penalties are run together wrongly. The statute: stocks for three days or more for those who refuse; forty days' prison for a first conviction for taking too much; a quarter of a year for the second.
19. Line 68: "ride from town to town" is invented. The source says sessions.
20. Line 71: the sum is £84 7s 7d, raised by the justices at Colchester. "A lot of days" is vague; the arithmetic is 20,251 pence.
21. Line 75: "Someone was checking" asserts a reading the historian does not make. He says the crossing-out may be an attempt to get round the law. Give both readings.
22. Line 77: "Better food" is not in the source. It says more and larger tips in cash and perquisites in kind.
23. Line 78: "quietly changes to wheat" overstates. The source: the abbot paid seasonal rewards and grain stipends in wheat, "no doubt" to get round the limits. That is the historian's inference.
24. Line 79: "Most of them" needs its basis: one study, eight manors, seven with no cases.
25. Line 80: no source opened says lords were fined and "overpaid anyway" as a pattern. Knighton says the king fined abbots, priors and knights; a 2023 paper says employers were often breaking the rules by the 1360s. Not needed; cut the line.
26. Line 83: the 1363 law applied to grooms, servants of lords and craftsmen; "and no more" is my addition. The clothing rule for ploughmen was found only on a page that cites no source, so it is left out.
27. Line 92: "doubled" should be "nearly doubled" (0.13s to 0.25s).
28. Line 93: "written to pin down" claims intent. What the text does: it singles out the shortage of ploughmen.
29. Line 96: 19 to 29 shillings before, 56 shillings by the 1430s: "roughly doubled".
30. Lines 98-99: the script jumps from "what changed in the 1380s" to the Peasants' Revolt as if the revolt raised wages. The researchers' own explanation is different: cheap land and day work gave workers options, grain prices fell and steadied so grain pay mattered less, and employers stopped holding the line. Give their explanation, then the revolt as a separate event.
31. Line 100: "Thousands" was not in a source opened. Cut the number.
32. Line 101: the king's age was not in a source opened. Cut. The wage-limit demand is in the list of rebel demands generally, not tied to Mile End.
33. Line 102: Tyler was stabbed by the mayor of London, then executed. "Cut down in front of him" is loose.
34. Line 103: the charters were revoked on 2 July. "Torn up" is colour. The 1,500 are "executed or killed in battle".
35. Line 104: "almost everything they asked for" is too strong, and it implies the revolt caused it. The source says serfdom declined "primarily for economic rather than political reasons".
36. Line 106: "quietly" is invented.
37. Line 107: "never abolished" is an absolute nobody checked. What is supported: the charters were revoked, and serfdom "withered away" over the fifteenth century.
38. Line 110: contradicts line 95. The law did help hold the ploughman's pay flat for about thirty years.
39. Line 113: "nobody could remember" is a flourish with no source. Cut.

## Second reader on v2 (independent fact gate, same day)

A separate reviewer compared every line of v2 with the quoted evidence in `facts.json` and re-opened six sources (Fordham, EH.net, Goose, LSE working paper 360, Bailey 2025, Wikipedia on the revolt). Arithmetic checked: £84 7s 7d = 20,251d; 0.13s = 1.56d; 0.25s = 3d. Changes made to `EP004_script_v2_final.txt` as a result:

1. "There isn't another poll tax for six hundred years" removed. Only the London Museum page says it, and the reviewer doubted it. The line now says Parliament did not try another poll tax (Wikipedia's wording).
2. The Suffolk reaper's tips moved up to the 1349 harvest, where the source puts them. They are no longer presented as a dodge round the 1351 statute.
3. The Ordinance (June 1349) came before Knighton's autumn reaper. The script now says "the king has already moved" and the cold open says "within the year".
4. "The law is beaten" now says "for day wages", and the close says the law was "part of the reason" pay stayed down, so the two lines no longer contradict each other.
5. The golden age is attributed to the three authors; "half the people are gone" became "perhaps half"; the 1361 toll is "by one estimate".
6. The coin part of the pay is given as a range (five to ten shillings), because the Kent manor in the same script paid its ploughmen 9s to 11s 6d.
7. "Everyone does", "many of your neighbours" and "report each other" were quantifiers with no source: now "plenty of others", "some", "every town has a reason to turn offenders in".
8. The ship from Gascony is attributed to one chronicle. The wage-limit demand of 1381 is attributed to "one account". The abbot's motive is "one historian has no doubt why".
9. Reaping: the statute's 3d runs from the second week to the end of August, and Knighton's 8d included food; both now said.
10. Kent manor: the roll's reason is attached to the cash pay, as in the source; the food increase was not all kept and the script says so; "on average" added to the thirty-year finding.
11. The 1388 statute is described as Parliament giving ground on pay (higher caps), not as the rebels getting what they asked for. "In the middle of all that" became "just as that begins".
12. The 1363 rule is worded as a limit ("only once a day").

Left as they are, with the reviewer's agreement or after a second look: the stocks and forty-day penalties (the clause was re-read word for word); the Warboys list of 22 tenants kept by Ramsey Abbey's monks (confirmed on the Durham page in this run).

Length after changes: 119 lines, about 1,660 words. At EP003's pace (1,360 words in 6:53) that is about 8 minutes 25 seconds.
