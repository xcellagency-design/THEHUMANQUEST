### MODERATE

**1. Row 22.** "How did it come to this? Germany paid for the war mostly by borrowing. Then came the reparations bill. And the state found the money it needed by printing more of its own."
- Note (F4): each sentence has its own fragment. The note itself says "no fragment says reparations caused the printing: the three sentences are a sequence, not a cause", and that what "dafür" refers to in the Bundestag sentence was not returned.
- Problem: under the question "How did it come to this?", joined by "Then" and "And", the ear hears "it printed money to pay the reparations". The order of sentences makes a cause the notes do not support.
- Rewrite: "How did it come to this? Three things, in order. Germany paid for the war mostly by borrowing. Then came a reparations bill. And the state, short of funds, multiplied its own paper money."
- Better still: re-open the Bundestag page, read what "dafür" points at, and say that.

**2. Rows 59 and 86.** "That week the British ambassador does a sum. A pound costs sixty billion marks. About one mark, he notes, for every second since the birth of Christ."
- Note (F24, qualified, Fergusson): "On October 21, after the mark had moved in three days from the 24 milliards to 80 milliards to the pound", he noted that "(at 60 milliards)" this was about one mark per second.
- Three problems:
  - Sixty billion is the figure he used for the sum, inside a three-day swing from 24 to 80. The row states it as the price of the pound.
  - 21 October was a Sunday, two days before the 23 October letter this row follows. "That week" puts it after the letter.
  - It is a popular history quoting him, with his own text not found. The same book gets "by one account" in row 35 and no signal here or in row 86.
- Rewrite row 59: "Two days earlier, as one history quotes him, the British ambassador does a sum. At sixty billion marks to the pound, that's about one mark for every second since the birth of Christ."
- Row 86's words match the fragment. Once row 59 carries the signal, "the British ambassador writes" inherits it; otherwise add "as the same history quotes him".

**3. Rows 72 and 73.** "One economist wrote the rule down: contract as many debts as possible."

- Problem: the row turns it into a timeless rule in the imperative. It is the one line in the script that can be heard as present-day financial advice, and certainly would be if clipped for a Short. Rows 21, 44 and 67 are safe because they carry "nineteen twenty-three" or "marks".
- Second problem: row 73 (Stinnes) follows directly, so the order says he grew by borrowing. The note says that is not established.
- Rewrite row 72: "One economist wrote that in those years it became a rule of good management: contract as many debts as possible."
- Row 73: no change of words. Do not draw Stinnes borrowing, and do not clip rows 72 and 73 as a pair.
