## What the research changed
- **The plan was "week by week".** The good weekly data exist only for the last two months. The spine is now the savings book counted in bread: about 34,000 kilos in 1913, 40 in January, 7 in June, not quite 3 in July, a seventh of a kilo in August, a crumb in September.
- **Most of the famous stories have no source that could be opened.** The wheelbarrow of cash for a loaf: a figure of speech in two popular books. The thief who left the money: already "a current story in Berlin" in early 1923, in a newspaper quoted at second hand. The coffee that cost more by the time it was drunk: four versions with four sets of numbers, and one dated first-hand original, in which the waiter lets the customer pay the old price. Children playing with bundles, kites, the woman at the stove: undated agency captions. Pay twice a day and wives at the factory gates: not found. The script uses what could be read: handcarts for wages, the coffee as Klemperer wrote it down, the caption about writing on the back of a million-mark note.
- **"Everyone was paid daily" is wrong.** Daily pay was common; a Berlin magazine in late August and a Berlin print shop in mid-October still speak of the week's wage. The script says "Not everyone."
- **The winners are the story.** A Bavarian historical encyclopedia, the German Historical Museum and two economists say the same thing: the gain went to borrowers, the state first. The hidden quest is built on that, with its limits (rent control; a 1925 law that brought back a quarter of old mortgages).
- **Savers did not get nothing.** From 1925 there was a legal floor for savings-bank deposits of about an eighth. The episode ends on it.
- **1923 was not what put Hitler in power, and the script does not say it played no part.** The Holocaust Memorial Museum's sentence and the 1928 vote are given; Stefan Zweig is named for the opposite view.
- **German number words.** Milliarde is billion and Billion is trillion; one page of the Bundestag's own site gives 1922 dollar prices that are really gold-mark prices. Every figure in the fact table says which scale it is in.
