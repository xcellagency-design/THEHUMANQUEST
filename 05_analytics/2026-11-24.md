## What the evidence supports
1. **Changing the opening words did not change the first 20 seconds.** EP001 opened on a myth and a source; EP003 opened in second person with a clock and a count of the dead. Both lost about half their viewers by 0:20 and about three in five by 0:35, and both lost a quarter inside 8 seconds, before two sentences were out. Friday's note blamed EP001's script for the half that left by 0:20. With a second curve of the same shape under a different script, that reading is weaker: the loss sits in something the two videos share. They share the first frames (EP003's first frame is a text card, "POMPEII. AD 79."), the flat stills, the voice, and whoever YouTube showed them to. This data cannot say which. It is consistent with the owner's complaint about the animation and does not prove it.
2. **The quest log did not lose anyone.** From 0:25 to 0:37, while the three objectives were read, the line is flat. The next loss (three viewers of seven) came between 1:06 and 1:23, in the first passage that describes the town rather than doing something to "you".
3. **EP003's middle held a little better than EP001's**: 19% to 24% still watching between 2:00 and 4:00 against 14% to 16%, and about typical for its length between 30% and 46% of the way in, where EP001 was below typical. EP003 has a clock and a decision coming; EP001 did not. EP003's ending did worse (0.20 against 0.69). One video each, one or two viewers' difference: a lead.
4. **Second Short far above the rest: "Most People in Pompeii Got Out Alive", 319 views in two and a half days**, close to the wet-sand Short's 349 in eight. Its first words: "For a long time, the story of Pompeii ended right there. Everybody died. They didn't." The title corrects what everyone believes about a famous event. The wet-sand title names a trick. The two weakest by reach are "Election Ads" (5 views in four and a half days) and "You Were Lied To" (2): one opens on a description with nothing at stake, the other accuses the viewer. Four videos, and the feed decides reach before any viewer does, so this is a lead for titles and first lines, not a rule.
5. **The 1929 Shorts are the best-held on the channel and it has not bought them reach.** Mattress 84%, Margin 82%, Every Bank 70% average viewed; 75, 33 and 97 views. The wet-sand Short (123%) is the only case where the best-held is also the most seen. Holding the viewers who arrive and being shown to more of them look like separate problems here.
6. **A Short's life on this channel is about three days.** The nine pyramid Shorts gained 8 views between them since Friday. Nothing published a week ago is still being shown.
7. **About a quarter to a third of Short plays turn into an engaged view** (105 of 349, 79 of 304, 65 of 237, 20 of 76). The rest are swiped past at the start. No benchmark for this was looked up, so no judgement on whether that is low.
8. **Long videos are still hardly shown: 52, 26 and 1.** EP002's long video has had one view in five days. With impressions unavailable, low reach and low click-through cannot be told apart. The Shorts are not visibly sending viewers to them, and the traffic-source report that would show it was not pulled.

Confidence: **moderate** that the first-20-second loss is not about the wording of the hook (two curves, same shape, different scripts, 64 viewers in all). **Low** on everything else.
