## What the research changed
- **The plan was "what winter was for": the sail gets made in winter. No source says that, and one says cloth was made all year round.** The hidden quest is now: the work that does not stop for the season, and who did it. The numbers are the episode's strongest material and they disagree with each other, so the script gives a range and names the kind of estimate: a year's wool from 150 to 700 sheep for one sail; four women for a year at ten hours a day by one experiment-based estimate; by one calculation, 84 person-years of textile work to equip the biggest warship, with building the ship about 3% of the job.
- **A link nobody in the sources makes, kept as two facts side by side:** the Danish experiment's authors think women and small children probably breathed the most smoke; the University of Bergen says textile work was women's work. The script says both and lets the viewer join them.
- **"Viking" is partly a seasonal job, and the evidence is one saga and one chronicle entry.** Orkneyinga saga has Svein Asleifarson sowing in spring, raiding, home for the harvest, raiding again, and wintering at home with eighty men. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle notes in 851 that the heathens stayed over winter "for the first time". The script gives both and does not make a rule of them.
- **Almost none of the evidence is from Norway in 950.** The calendar is Icelandic and first attested in the 12th century; the farming evidence is Icelandic; the houses are Danish; the skates, the sock and the lighting are from York; Yule is from an Icelander writing about 1230. Line 5 says so, and each passage names its place.
- **Yule is thin.** The one detailed account is centuries late and its date is disputed; one source says most popular claims about Yule are unsupported. The script says "not much" and keeps to the ale law and the horse liver, both attributed.
